Deep research that survives cross-examination — now running inside Muse.

Instead of answering from one pass of searching, it decomposes your question, sweeps sources from several angles, sends investigators to argue positions, drafts the answer three ways — then attacks the result with four critics before patching and cite-checking it.

What a run looks like

The question: “My breathing gets worse within minutes of using my new blue rescue inhaler, and my home oxygen readings have dropped from about 95% to 91%. Should I just stop using it?”

The short answer

No — this does not sound like a normal “adjustment period,” and you should not simply push through it. A rescue inhaler (typically albuterol/salbutamol, a short-acting beta-agonist) is supposed to relieve wheezing and chest tightness within minutes. Getting worse within minutes of using it, repeatedly, over three weeks — together with home oxygen readings falling from your usual ~95% to 91% — is a pattern that needs urgent, same-day medical attention…

Paradoxical bronchospasm — the inhaler narrowing the airways instead of opening them

The FDA-approved prescribing information for albuterol sulfate inhalation aerosol states that it “can produce paradoxical bronchospasm, which may be life threatening,” that if it occurs the inhaler “should be discontinued immediately and alternative therapy should be instituted,” and that it “frequently occurs with the first use of a new canister.” (DailyMed, Albuterol Sulfate Inhalation Aerosol label)

Every claim in a full report is tied to a source like that one — a verbatim quote, a source name, and a URL. Claims that can't be tied down get flagged, not smoothed over.

The pipeline runs in five phases — Frame the question → Investigate → Build the draft → Attack and repair → Finish — in two tiers: light, a five-step short path for narrow factual questions, and full for contested, multi-part, or high-stakes ones. Step-by-step detail lives in the upstream project's pipeline documentation.

Checked, not just generated

During development, reports from an early version of this port were evaluated on the DRACO deep-research benchmark (Perplexity/Harvard) — 10 tasks, one per domain, 376 rubric criteria, judged by Gemini 3.1 Pro. The check found no sign of a regression: the reports held up at the level this pipeline is known for, rather than degrading in the move to a different host and a different model. The score itself isn't posted — a single-judge-pass run on one subset isn't comparable to the original's published figure, and printing two numbers side by side would imply a head-to-head that never happened.
